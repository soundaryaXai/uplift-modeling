    The idea

    Persuadables
    Act only if contacted. The only group worth paying for.
    Sure Things
    Act either way. Contacting them wastes spend.
    Lost Causes
    Won't act either way.
    Sleeping Dogs
    Act less if contacted.

    Why the comparison is fair

    In the real datasets, each customer was randomly chosen to be contacted or held back. Random assignment makes the two groups alike in everything except the contact, so a gap in outcomes can be read as the effect of the contact.

    The balance check under "Can I trust this?" tests that: if customer features could predict who was contacted, randomization would be in doubt.

    What it cannot tell you

    Nobody is ever both contacted and not contacted, so on real data an individual's effect is never seen. The scores measure how well a ranking sorts groups of customers, not how close each estimate is to a hidden truth. That is what the synthetic dataset is for.

    Results are about visits or conversions, not profit. Neither real dataset includes contact costs, so the cost above is your assumption.
